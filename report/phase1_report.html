Does the Raspberry Pi 5 radiate a data-dependent signal?

A first bring-up with a handheld spectrum analyzer: can we detect, above a noisy unshielded room, an RF emission that switches with the board's memory activity — and at what frequency?

2026-10-17R. BoadaAdv. S. Sethumadhavan tinySA Ultra+ · whip · no shield

Short answer: yes — but not where we first looked. A single on/off spectrum pair pointed at ~18 MHz, but that turned out to be ambient shortwave. With proper averaging the board's own activity-dependent emission shows up cleanly in UHF, at 263.4 and 286.8 MHz.

  • Cautionary result The apparent ~18 MHz "emission" from single on/off differencing was ambient HF (the 15–20 MHz shortwave/ham band), not the Pi. It vanishes under averaging.
  • Primary result Averaged on/off differencing reveals a genuine activity-dependent emission at 263.4 MHz and 286.8 MHz — detections at t ≈ 8–10 (4–5σ), reproducible across 12 on/off cycles.
  • Method The board-vs-ambient distinction is only visible with averaging + significance testing; it is the key technique for every later phase (and the argument for the shield).

01Why a single measurement isn't enough

The plan was to prove the board radiates by differencing the spectrum with the Pi's memory loop running (./tx_ddr --carrier) against idle. A single on/off pair showed strong +10 dB "lines" near 18 MHz — convincing until you repeat it. The room is unshielded, and 15–20 MHz is full of intermittent shortwave broadcast and amateur signals that a whip antenna picks up. A single pair catches one of those in the "on" sweep and calls it signal.

02The real emission, in UHF

Sweeping higher bands with the same averaged, carrier-toggled differencing and a per-bin Welch t-test, two narrow lines stand out far above the noise — and only these two. They sit at 263.4 MHz and 286.8 MHz, consistent with the Pi 5's high-speed clock/switching activity rather than the low-frequency memory "tick" we first aimed for.

03Watching it switch — waterfall

Zooming the analyzer onto the 286.8 MHz line and sweeping repeatedly over time, with the memory loop on for the first 20 sweeps and off for the last 20, shows the line brighten and dim in step with the board's activity.

04What this means & next steps

Phase 1's goal — show the board emits a measurable, data-dependent RF signal — is met, at 263.4 / 286.8 MHz. The emission is amplitude-modulated by activity, so the natural modulation scheme here is on/off keying (OOK) at those carriers, not the low-MHz frequency-shift scheme we started with (the memory tick rate barely radiates on this hardware). The absolute levels are low (−72 to −77 dBm at a bare whip in open air), which is why averaging was essential.

Two corrections worth recording: (1) the initial 18 MHz result did not survive averaging and was ambient — flagged before any conclusion left the bench; (2) a bug in the transmitter's frequency parser (SI suffixes like 1M were read as 1 Hz) was found and fixed. The HackRF antenna was tried on the analyzer and performed worse than the stock whip at these frequencies, so the whip was kept.
